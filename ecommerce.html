The Safety Audit Comes After the Trucks Are Already Rolling. A Long-Dormant Rule Asks Whether That's Backwards.

The FMCSA is revisiting a 17-year-dormant rulemaking that would require new motor carriers to pass a safety proficiency exam before receiving operating authority, rather than self-certifying knowledge on a form and undergoing audits after 18 months of operation. The rule, originally mandated by Congress in 1999 but never fully implemented, targets a supplemental advance notice for November 2026, addressing a structural gap where carriers can legally operate 80,000-pound vehicles on public highways before demonstrating actual safety competency.
